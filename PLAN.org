#+title: Antenna Turtle Future Development Plan

* Purpose

This file is the parking place for capabilities deliberately deferred beyond
the first solid-modeling/OpenEMS revision.  It is an idea inventory, not a
promise of order or scope.  Concrete release work and defects remain in
~TODO.org~; completed Revision 1 features are documented in the manual and API
reference rather than repeated here.

The broad direction is to preserve the small turtle vocabulary while adding
physical modeling only where a compact, composable idiom is clear.  Solver
details should remain behind solver-neutral scene and measurement objects
whenever practical.

* Likely next milestones

** Planar and distributed ports

The first OpenEMS backend has axis-aligned lumped ports.  Add explicit planar
ports with the geometry and modal information needed for:

- microstrip and stripline;
- coplanar structures;
- coaxial lines;
- rectangular or other supported waveguides; and
- general modal/distributed-port measurements.

The API should use programmer-controlled port names, retain the existing
multi-excitation result model, and make reference planes and polarity
unambiguous.  A useful first target is a microstrip port that is less sensitive
to the dimensions of a lumped excitation block.

** Topology-aware mesh simplification

Replace purely conservative refinement with an iterative, geometry-aware
plane-removal pass.  It should operate on the three axis-line sets while:

- preserving ports, explicit ~mesh-point~ and ~mesh-plane~ constraints,
  material boundaries, and required metal-edge lines;
- checking material occupancy, topology, and allowed geometric error;
- enforcing wavelength resolution, adjacent-cell grading, aspect-ratio, and
  total-cell limits; and
- explaining retained or removed critical lines in the manifest.

This is the main route to smaller simulations without making the mesher
silently change the electrical model.

** Further broadband and magnetic materials

Revision 1 now supports model-centered and explicitly ranged causal broadband
dielectric fits plus explicit Debye relaxation poles. Later material support
should investigate:

- fitting measured multi-frequency complex-permittivity data rather than one
  nominal permittivity/loss-tangent point;
- volumetric or dispersive conductor models for curved and oblique metal,
  instead of restricting finite-conductivity bodies to Yee-aligned faces;
- complex and frequency-dependent permeability;
- anisotropic and gyrotropic media; and
- ferrite presets only after the backend can represent their magnetic
  behavior honestly.

The room-temperature material catalog should remain convenient defaults, not
a substitute for frequency-specific vendor data.  Temperature modeling is not
currently a goal.

** Better power accounting

The current power budget reports radiation, dielectric heat, resistive
lumped-element heat, delivered port power, and a conductor/numerical
remainder.  Improve this by measuring conductor dissipation directly enough to
separate it from PML, discretization, and conservation-closure error.

The current dielectric subtotal includes narrowband conductivity loss but not
Debye polarization-relaxation heat. Add frequency-domain field integration for
dispersive dielectrics before presenting that heat as a measured category.

Breakdowns by useful material class or material (conductors, dielectrics,
lumped resistors, radiation, and ports) are desirable.  Per-object heat maps
or a line item for every component are not a priority unless a concrete
diagnostic need appears.

** Additional manufacturing importers

Build on STL import with formats that naturally describe RF fabrication:

- extruded DXF outlines; and
- PCB artwork such as Gerber copper layers.

Keep parsing and scene construction independent of OpenEMS so imported models
can be reused by later backends.  Layer mapping, units, thickness, material,
and precedence must be explicit and reproducible.

* Solid construction beyond Revision 1

** PEC enclosures and CSG boundary robustness

The staged enclosure policy is:

1. Revision 1 guarantees at least two cells across every bounding-box
   dimension of an explicit ~form solid~ PEC body.  Construct cavity walls as
   separate, possibly overlapping cuboids when using this path.  A hollow
   ~difference~ body does not reveal wall thickness through its final bounding
   box, so this guarantee deliberately does not claim to repair that model.
2. The opt-in ~form surface~ path converts the final axis-aligned Manifold
   boundary to native PEC polygons.  It handles the inner and outer faces of a
   Boolean-difference cavity, forces PEC when material is omitted, and rejects
   explicit non-PEC material.
3. A future revision should make surface conversion robust for more complex
   geometry.  Topics include coplanar triangle merging, removal of internal
   faces in touching or overlapping assemblies, consistent surface normals,
   oblique facets and their Yee-grid approximation, and ray/occupancy tests
   when final-boundary topology alone is insufficient.  If CSG source-feature
   provenance is retained, it may also guide critical mesh coordinates, but
   those coordinates must correspond to surviving physical boundaries rather
   than blindly preserving every cutter plane.

The STL solid path remains available because callers may intentionally want a
volumetric PEC object or arbitrary geometry.  The surface path therefore stays
an explicit ~form surface~ choice rather than becoming an automatic PEC
optimization.

** Turtle-path solids

Add a path-construction context distinct from ~with-traces~.  Candidate
operations are:

- trace a closed outline in the turtle's local XY plane and extrude it along
  turtle up;
- revolve a closed trace around an explicit axis;
- sweep a circular, rectangular, or arbitrary profile along a turtle path;
- loft between two or more traces; and
- decide explicitly whether a constructed solid also contributes a useful
  centerline to NEC-style wire exporters.

Planar extrusion is the smallest coherent first slice: reject out-of-plane
movement, require a closed valid outline, and specify extrusion direction and
distance without exposing raw Manifold objects.

** Reserved CSG and transformation operations

The following Manifold-style operations were considered useful but postponed
until real models establish their exact turtle semantics:

- ~stretch~ for independent forward/back, left/right, and up/down scaling;
- mirror about an explicit axis/plane and origin;
- trim with an explicit plane and a clear choice of which side survives;
- split a body by a plane;
- Minkowski sum; and
- more general extrusion operations.

Uniform body ~scale~, ~union~, ~difference~, ~intersect~, and ~hull~ already
cover Revision 1.  New operations should work inside ~with-body~ and preserve
the established anchor and turtle-frame rules.

** Trace refinements

Revision 1 traces are planar constant-width sheets with butt ends and miter or
bevel joins.  Possible later additions include:

- selectable end caps and corner profiles;
- tapered or varying-width paths;
- an explicit way to join or loft adjacent ~with-traces~ scopes; and
- a generalized nonplanar trace or ribbon only if a compelling RF use case
  appears.

Axis-aligned traces should continue to receive ideal metal-edge meshing, while
oblique traces use controlled width refinement.

** Automated material Boolean resolution

Revision 1 requires explicit precedence for unlike overlapping materials.
Future work may define safe Boolean ownership rules for selected combinations,
but must not guess when the intended physical interface is ambiguous.

* Components and network simulation

** Measured multiport component substitution

Investigate embedding a measured component described by Touchstone S
parameters.  The motivating use is a realistic packaged RF component with
parasitics beyond an ideal series/parallel RLC model.  A two-port network has
more terminal and reference-plane semantics than the current two-terminal
~lumped-element~, and OpenEMS may not provide a direct implementation; keep
this item backend-dependent rather than faking it as an ideal lumped element.

** Mixed reference impedances

The OpenEMS multiport result currently assumes one common reference
impedance.  Add correct mixed-impedance power-wave normalization, preserving
named ports and the solver-neutral S-parameter API.

** Optional frequency-domain NF2FF recording

Time-domain recording is the Revision 1 default because it permits arbitrary
in-band postprocessing.  Consider an explicit list of recorded frequencies for
smaller artifacts when the user accepts that other frequencies cannot be
recovered without rerunning FDTD.

* Grounds and boundaries

** Infinite-ground modeling for field solvers

Consider a clear model-level metaphor for a monopole or other structure over
an infinite PEC ground when the backend can implement it faithfully through a
boundary condition or image symmetry.  Do not represent infinity with an
arbitrarily large hidden sheet.

** NEC radial ground screens

Extend the existing free-space, perfect-ground, and real-ground NEC support
with optional radial-wire ground screens and explicit dimensions/material
parameters.

* Solver and exporter growth

** Palace or another finite-element backend

Preserve solver-neutral bodies, materials, ports, manifests, and measurement
results so a future Palace/FEM backend can reuse the DSL.  Importers should not
be coupled to CSXCAD, and backend-specific restrictions should remain
validation rules rather than leak into basic geometry construction.

** Solid-aware MATLAB export

Add a physical-solid MATLAB export path rather than silently reducing solid
models to wire ribbons or lofts.  Define the supported material and port
subset explicitly.

** Exporter diagnostics and parity

Warn when wire-only exporters such as NEC2C or MMANA ignore explicit bodies or
sheets.  Continue improving branch handling in the approximate MATLAB loft
exporter if a stable solid topology can be represented.

* Measurement and automation

** Coalesced simulation requests

Add a batch request API capable of combining compatible impedance, sweep,
pattern, and other measurements into the fewest NEC2C or OpenEMS executions.
The independent measurement procedures should remain the simple public entry
points; batching must be an optimization, not a requirement for ordinary use.

** Optimization conveniences

Possible additions for automated design searches include:

- serialization of result data to CSV, JSON, or s-expressions;
- bandwidth, beamwidth, efficiency, and other summary metrics not already in
  the measurement-query API;
- small composable fitness helpers; and
- controlled parallel evaluation with bounded solver concurrency and
  deterministic artifact ownership.

Raw transparent result structures and ordinary Racket iteration must remain
available so specialized optimization never depends on a large framework.

** Stronger reference verification

Turn the verification examples into durable numerical references after each
model has been manually inspected.  Record expected quantities and defensible
tolerances, exercise multiple mesh profiles where appropriate, and keep long
external-solver checks opt-in until their runtime is suitable for routine CI.

* Visualization and live-workflow improvements

** Reusable handles for Plot and Plot3D

OpenGL radiation views already support in-place replacement through
~visualization-handle~.  Extend that lifecycle to ordinary Plot and Plot3D
windows so parameter searches can update existing graphs while preserving
window placement, zoom, and presentation state.

** Deliberate display of triangulated geometry

Revision 1 normally hides internal polygon triangulation because it distracts
from the authoritative outline.  A future diagnostic view may expose triangles
and solver primitives on demand, together with mesh constraints, without
making them the normal solid-model presentation.

* General turtle-language ideas

** Recorded coordinates and vector-directed turns

Consider named/recorded geometric coordinates and an operation that turns the
turtle toward a vector derived from recorded points or a recorded segment.
The design must distinguish coordinate data from turtle state and compose
cleanly with ~here~, ~goto~, ~from-here~, scaling, and reflection.

** Generalized file libraries

Racket's ~require~ and ~provide~ already support reusable libraries of turtle
geometry, so no separate include form is planned.  Revisit this only if model
authors encounter a concrete problem that modules cannot solve cleanly.

* Ideas intentionally parked pending a use case

These are remembered but should not drive an API merely because Manifold or a
solver happens to expose them:

- arbitrary three-dimensional PCB traces;
- plane splitting, trimming, or Minkowski operations without a model that
  demonstrates readable turtle syntax;
- temperature-dependent material presets;
- individual-component heat accounting; and
- hidden approximations for unsupported infinite geometry or network
  components.

When one of these becomes necessary, begin with the model that requires it and
design the smallest operation that expresses that model naturally.
